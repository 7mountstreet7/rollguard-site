What it is (150 words)

RollGuard is a privacy app that scans an iPhone's photo library for the sensitive things everyone photographs and forgets: identity documents, payment cards, screenshots of passwords and credentials, and private images. Every scan runs entirely on-device — photos are never uploaded, no account exists, and scanning works in Airplane Mode. Machine-readable documents are read and checked against their own rules; anything RollGuard can't verify is still flagged, labelled honestly as "possible". The free tier is a complete product — every flag in the most recent 30 days plus a running count across the whole library — and RollGuard Pro opens the review queue for everything beyond the window — every flagged item with its thumbnail, each one yours to keep, hide, or delete — for a single US$19.99 payment. No subscription, ever. Before launch, the codebase was audited against every public claim — each promise traced to a line of code. RollGuard never tells you your library is "safe." It flags; you decide.

Coverage

How RollGuard reads identity documents

  • RollGuard doesn't just spot things that look like documents — it reads them. Passports and modern national ID cards carry machine-readable numbers with their own built-in arithmetic, and RollGuard checks that arithmetic. Passports from 36 countries have verified this way in testing against real specimen documents — and because it's arithmetic, not translation, it works whatever language the document is printed in.
  • Deep, specimen-tested coverage for Australia, New Zealand, the UK, Ireland, Canada and the US — passports, driving licences, Medicare and health cards, birth certificates, working-with-children checks and more — with broad coverage across Europe on top.
  • 40 countries have at least one detection proven against a real specimen document, and RollGuard recognises document titles in more than 25 languages — including right-to-left scripts such as Dari and Pashto.
  • National identifiers that publish a check digit — Australia's Medicare and Tax File Number, New Zealand's IRD number, the UK's NHS number, Ireland's PPS number, Canada's SIN — are validated with the real arithmetic, never guessed.
  • Screenshots of crypto wallet recovery phrases are checked against the real BIP-39 wordlist and checksum.
  • When something looks like an identity document but can't be verified, RollGuard still flags it — labelled "possible" — and you decide. RollGuard never tells you your library is "safe."
