Accessibility

What RollGuard does to be usable with iOS's accessibility features — and what we haven't done.

In the app

RollGuard is built with the standard iOS interface components, so it inherits the system's accessibility behaviour rather than reimplementing it. Specifically:

  • VoiceOver. Controls carry labels, and the ones whose purpose isn't obvious from the label carry a hint as well. Where a control sits next to a count — "398 checked · 6 flagged" — the label reads the numbers and the hint gives the action, so a VoiceOver user hears the same information a sighted user sees rather than just the button name.
  • Dynamic Type. Text scales with your iPhone's text-size setting; RollGuard never opts out of scaling. Where a large text size has pushed a control off screen, we've treated that as a bug and fixed it.
  • Reduce Motion. If Reduce Motion is on, RollGuard's animated progress indicator stops animating and shows a static form instead. The app reads the setting at launch and follows it if you change it while the app is open.
  • Appearance. The app follows your system light or dark setting.

On this website

The site is plain HTML with no third-party scripts. Text scales with your browser's text-size setting, the film is click-to-play rather than autoplaying, and animation is suppressed when your browser reports a Reduce Motion preference.

What we haven't done

We have not carried out a formal accessibility audit, and we don't claim conformance to a particular standard or level. RollGuard is made by one person, and stating a conformance level we haven't independently tested against would be a claim we can't support. What's described above is what the app and site actually do; it isn't a certification, and it isn't exhaustive.

We also know of no way to make one part of the app fully accessible: reviewing a flagged photo means looking at the photo. VoiceOver will read the category, the reason and the masked fragment RollGuard stored, and the Keep, Hide and Delete controls are fully labelled — but the image itself is not described, because RollGuard never analyses image content beyond the checks it runs on your device.

Telling us about a problem

If something in RollGuard or on this site doesn't work with an accessibility feature you rely on, please email support@rollguard.app and say which feature and which screen. It goes to the person who wrote the app. We'll tell you honestly whether we can fix it and roughly when.
